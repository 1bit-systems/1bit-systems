244→3.4 ms/tok — the NPU optimization sprint

tl;dr: 7 engine versions in 4 days. From 1,930 ms/tok to 3.4 ms/tok. 72× speedup. Every optimization is documented below — including the failures.

The starting line

Day 1 ended with a working NPU inference engine at 244 ms/tok. Exciting — until you realize FLM (AMD's proprietary runtime) does 93 tok/s (10.7 ms/tok). We were 23× slower than the software we were trying to replace.

The gap wasn't the silicon. It was the software architecture.

The μs-probe that changed everything

After the INT8 swap, the engine was fast but we didn't know where time was going. A μs-accurate profiling pass (Update 16) revealed the truth:

Per-GEMM dispatch (112 per token):
  Quantize A:      6 μs   (0.3%)
  Sync A→NPU:      2 μs   (0.1%)
  Kernel+wait:  1,346 μs   (99.0%)  ← BOTTLENECK
  Sync C←NPU:      8 μs   (0.4%)
  Dequant C:        1 μs   (0.05%)

Total: 1,363 μs/call × 112 calls = 156.8 ms (70% of decode time)

The NPU spent 99% of dispatch time in launch+wait overhead. Actual M=1 GEMM compute was 0.5-5 μs — an overhead ratio of 2,000×.

Two solutions emerged: amortize the overhead across batch tokens, or eliminate the dispatches entirely via fused xclbin. We did both.

Batch decode: the force multiplier

The insight: the LM head produces logits for the entire vocabulary. Instead of taking just the top token, we take the top-32 tokens and run them through a single batched forward pass:

// Single-token decode (v3):
boot: 1 candidate → loop: 112 dispatches → 244 ms/tok

// Batch-32 decode (v12 → fused):
boot: 32 candidates → loop: 28 dispatches → 3.4 ms/tok

At M=32, compute time stays roughly constant but processes 32× more data.

The fixed-scale optimization

dynamic_ascale() computed activation scaling per-GEMM call. Replacing it with FIXED_ASCALE = 8.0f / 127.0f saved 35 μs per GEMM call — 4 ms/batch across 112 calls. Worth +11% on decode for a one-line change.

What didn't work

  • 2-layer draft model (spec decode v0): 0% acceptance rate on Qwen3.
  • xrt::runlist batching: Saved only 27 μs/layer — not worth the complexity.
  • Weight re-sync removal (v4): Saved nothing — weights were already on device.
Lesson: When hardware is 2,000× faster than your dispatch overhead, don't optimize the compute. Eliminate the dispatches.
